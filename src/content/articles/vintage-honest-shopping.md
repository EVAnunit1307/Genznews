My grandmother has a coat older than my mother, and it will outlive me. I bought a jacket last winter that started shedding its lining by March. I keep thinking about those two facts next to each other, because I think they explain something the trend pieces keep missing.

Everyone wants to frame the secondhand boom as aesthetics — Gen Z discovering that old things look cool. That's not wrong, exactly. But it's the least interesting part of what's happening.

## The math changed, so the taste changed

Fast fashion made a bet: that we'd rather have ten cheap things than one good one. For a while that bet paid off. Then two things happened at once — the cheap things got *worse*, and we got poorer in the specific way where you can afford small purchases but never big ones.

> A $30 shirt that lasts four washes isn't cheap. It's a subscription you didn't know you signed up for.

Vintage broke that trap. Not because it's virtuous, but because a well-made jacket from 1994 has already proven it can survive thirty years. You're not gambling on quality. You're buying a receipt for it.

## This isn't about the planet (but it helps)

I want to be honest, because the sustainability framing does us no favors. Most of us didn't start thrifting to save the planet. We started because it was cheaper and the clothes were better made. The lower carbon footprint is real, but it's a side effect, not the motive.

That matters because side effects scale better than sacrifices. Ask people to shop ethically and a few will. Sell them a better jacket for less money that *happens* to be ethical, and you change an entire market.

- Better construction, already stress-tested by time
- Prices set by circumstance, not by a brand's margin
- Things nobody else at the party is wearing
- And yes — a lighter footprint you didn't have to feel guilty into

## What we're actually rejecting

The trend cycle wants you to believe taste is something you rent, month to month, and return when it expires. Vintage is the quiet refusal of that. It says taste is something you *keep*.

That's not nostalgia. Nostalgia is longing for a past you can't return to. This is the opposite — it's dragging the good parts of the past into the present because they still work, and refusing to apologize for it.
