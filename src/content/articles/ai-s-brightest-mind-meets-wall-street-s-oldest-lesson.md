### The Rise

Before becoming one of Wall Street's most talked-about investors, Aschenbrenner built a reputation in the AI world. A former OpenAI Superalignment researcher and former member of FTX's Future Fund, he gained widespread recognition after publishing his influential essay, _Situational Awareness_, which argued that artificial intelligence would rapidly transform the global economy. ([The Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/finance/leopold-aschenbrenner-situational-awareness-ai-fund-597633d3?utm_source=chatgpt.com "His Wedding Guests Were Arriving-Just as His $45 Billion Fund Was Falling Apart"))

Rather than simply writing about AI, he invested in it.

His fund made concentrated bets on the companies powering the AI revolution—semiconductor manufacturers, data-center infrastructure, electricity providers, and other businesses expected to benefit from explosive demand for computing power. The strategy generated extraordinary returns, attracting billions from investors convinced that AI would become the defining economic story of the decade. ([TIKR.com](https://www.tikr.com/blog/situational-awarenesss-portfolio-leopold-aschenbrenner?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Inside Situational Awareness: A Look at Leopold Aschenbrenner’s Top Stock Bets | TIKR.com"))

### What Went Wrong?

The investment thesis itself wasn't necessarily the problem.

The problem was leverage.

Reports indicate the fund borrowed heavily to amplify its positions. Leverage can supercharge gains when markets rise, but it works just as aggressively in reverse. When AI-related stocks sold off sharply in July, losses mounted rapidly, triggering margin calls from prime brokers. To meet those demands, the fund was forced into a large-scale liquidation of its public equity portfolio. ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/situational-awareness-portfolio-sinks-67-july-ai-stock-rout-letter-shows-2026-07-31/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Situational Awareness' portfolio sinks 67% in July on AI stock rout, letter shows"))

Many of those holdings were ultimately sold at a steep discount to **Ken Griffin's Citadel**, allowing the fund to raise cash before an even more severe collapse. Aschenbrenner later described the experience as resembling a "bank run," acknowledging that the firm's risk management had failed during a period of extreme market stress. ([The Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/finance/leopold-aschenbrenner-situational-awareness-ai-fund-597633d3?utm_source=chatgpt.com "His Wedding Guests Were Arriving-Just as His $45 Billion Fund Was Falling Apart"))

### Then Came the Wedding

As if Wall Street had a sense of irony, the financial turmoil peaked during Aschenbrenner's wedding weekend.

He married **Avital Balwit**, the **Chief of Staff to Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei**, in Carmel, California, while his fund was simultaneously fighting for survival. Guests gathered overlooking the Pacific as traders, brokers, and investors watched one of the most closely followed hedge fund unwinds in recent memory. ([Vanity Fair](https://www.vanityfair.com/story/anthropic-situational-awareness-wedding?utm_source=chatgpt.com "How to Lose Billions (and Gain a Wife) in Two Days"))

Most newlyweds worry about whether the flowers arrive on time.

Aschenbrenner reportedly had to worry about whether billions of dollars in assets would.

### Not the End of the Story

Despite sensational headlines, Aschenbrenner did **not** lose everything.

The fund retained valuable private investments—including its stake in Anthropic—which were not part of the forced liquidation. Those holdings remain among its most valuable assets, meaning the collapse was severe but far from total. Even after July's reported 67% decline, the fund remained positive for the year because of its earlier outsized gains. ([Vanity Fair](https://www.vanityfair.com/story/anthropic-situational-awareness-wedding?utm_source=chatgpt.com "How to Lose Billions (and Gain a Wife) in Two Days"))

### The Takeaway

If there's one lesson from the saga, it's that being right about the future isn't enough.

Aschenbrenner's belief that AI infrastructure would become one of the world's biggest investment opportunities may ultimately prove correct. But markets don't just reward good ideas—they reward investors who can survive long enough to see them play out.

Or, as Wall Street has taught generation after generation:

The market doesn't care how brilliant your thesis is if your leverage expires before your prediction comes true.
